@unit h1 | Vowels 1 | Hangul | Your first six Korean vowels
t Korean letters are grouped into syllable blocks. Every block starts with a consonant.
t ㅇ is silent at the start of a block, so 아 is just "a" and 오 is just "o".
t Vertical vowels (ㅏ ㅓ ㅣ) sit to the right of the consonant. Horizontal ones (ㅗ ㅜ ㅡ) sit below it.
l ㅏ | a | 아 | like "a" in "father"
l ㅓ | eo | 어 | like "u" in "fun"
l ㅗ | o | 오 | like "o" in "go", lips rounded
l ㅜ | u | 우 | like "oo" in "moon"
l ㅡ | eu | 으 | lips flat, tongue back, like "oo" without rounding
l ㅣ | i | 이 | like "ee" in "see"
w 아이 = child
w 오이 = cucumber
w 오 = five
w 이 = two

@unit h2 | Vowels 2 | Hangul | Y-vowels and the e-sounds
t Add one extra stroke and a vowel gets a "y" in front: ㅏ a → ㅑ ya, ㅗ o → ㅛ yo.
t ㅐ and ㅔ sound almost the same in modern Seoul Korean. You only need to tell them apart in spelling.
l ㅐ | ae | 애 | like "e" in "bed" | e
l ㅔ | e | 에 | almost the same as ㅐ | e
l ㅑ | ya | 야 | like "ya" in "yard"
l ㅕ | yeo | 여 | like "yu" in "young"
l ㅛ | yo | 요 | like "yo" in "yoga"
l ㅠ | yu | 유 | like "you"
w 우유 = milk
w 여우 = fox
w 이유 = reason

@unit h3 | Vowels 3 | Hangul | Combined vowels
t Combined vowels glide: ㅗ + ㅏ = ㅘ (wa), ㅜ + ㅓ = ㅝ (wo).
t ㅙ, ㅚ and ㅞ all sound like "we" today.
l ㅘ | wa | 와 | like "wa" in "water"
l ㅝ | wo | 워 | like "wo" in "won"
l ㅟ | wi | 위 | like "wee"
l ㅢ | ui | 의 | "eu" gliding into "i"
l ㅚ | oe | 외 | sounds like "we" | we
l ㅙ | wae | 왜 | sounds like "we" | we
l ㅖ | ye | 예 | like "ye" in "yes"
w 왜 = why
w 위 = above
w 예 = yes (formal)
w 와 = wow

@unit h4 | Consonants 1 | Hangul | The basic consonants

t ㄹ is a quick tap between vowels (like the "tt" in American "butter") and an "l" at the end of a syllable.
l ㄱ | g / k | 가 | soft "g" or "k"
l ㄴ | n | 나 | like "n"
l ㄷ | d / t | 다 | soft "d" or "t"

l ㅂ | b / p | 바 | soft "b" or "p"
l ㅅ | s | 사 | like "s"
l ㅈ | j | 자 | soft "j"
l ㅎ | h | 하 | like "h"

@unit h6 | Final consonants | Hangul | Batchim: consonants at the bottom
t A consonant at the bottom of a block is called 받침 (batchim).
t There are only seven final sounds: k, n, t, l, m, p, ng. Many letters collapse into these.
t ㅇ at the bottom is "ng", like in "sing".
l 악 | ak | 악 | final ㄱ: a short, unreleased "k"
l 안 | an | 안 | final ㄴ: "n"
l 앗 | at | 앗 | final ㅅ sounds like an unreleased "t"
l 알 | al | 알 | final ㄹ: a clear "l"
l 암 | am | 암 | final ㅁ: "m"
l 압 | ap | 압 | final ㅂ: an unreleased "p"
l 앙 | ang | 앙 | final ㅇ: "ng" as in "sing"

@unit u1 | Greetings | Basics | Say hello, thank you and goodbye
t 안녕하세요 works for hello at any time of day.
t There are two goodbyes: 안녕히 가세요 to someone who is leaving, 안녕히 계세요 to someone who is staying.
t 감사합니다 and 고맙습니다 both mean thank you. 감사합니다 is a little more formal.
w 안녕하세요 = hello
w 감사합니다 = thank you
w 네 = yes
w 아니요 = no
w 죄송합니다 = I'm sorry
w 괜찮아요 = it's okay
w 안녕히 가세요 = goodbye (to someone leaving)
w 안녕히 계세요 = goodbye (when you leave)
w 만나서 반가워요 = nice to meet you
w 저기요 = excuse me
s 안녕하세요, 만나서 반가워요. = Hello, nice to meet you.
s 네, 감사합니다. = Yes, thank you.
s 아니요, 괜찮아요. = No, it's okay.
s 죄송합니다, 괜찮아요? = I'm sorry, are you okay?
s 네, 괜찮아요. = Yes, it's okay.
s 감사합니다, 안녕히 계세요. = Thank you, goodbye.
s 정말 반가워요. = It's really nice to meet you.
s 아, 죄송합니다. = Oh, I'm sorry.
s 저기요, 괜찮아요? = Excuse me, are you okay?
s 고맙습니다, 내일 봐요. = Thanks, see you tomorrow.
f 만나서 [반가워요] = Nice to meet you. ; 감사합니다, 괜찮아요
f 감사합니다, 안녕히 [계세요] = Thank you, goodbye. ; 반가워요, 죄송합니다

@unit u2 | Introductions | Basics | Talk about who you are
t 저는 means "I" (as the topic). 은/는 marks the topic: 는 after a vowel, 은 after a consonant.
t "A is B" = A는 B이에요 / B예요. Use 이에요 after a consonant (학생이에요) and 예요 after a vowel (민수예요).
t To say "not", use 이/가 아니에요: 선생님이 아니에요.
w 저 = I (polite)
w 이름 = name
w 학생 = student
w 회사원 = office worker
w 사람 = person
w 우즈베키스탄 = Uzbekistan
w 친구 = friend
w 뭐 = what
w 나라 = country
w 의사 = doctor
s 저는 학생이에요. = I am a student.
s 제 이름은 민수예요. = My name is Minsu.
s 저는 우즈베키스탄 사람이에요. = I am from Uzbekistan.
s 이름이 뭐예요? = What is your name?
s 민수 씨는 한국 사람이에요. = Minsu is Korean.
s 저는 선생님이 아니에요. = I am not a teacher.
s 친구는 회사원이에요. = My friend is an office worker.
s 어느 나라 사람이에요? = Where are you from?
s 저도 학생이에요. = I am a student too.
s 제 친구는 의사예요. = My friend is a doctor.
f 저는 학생[이에요] = I am a student. ; 예요, 있어요
f 제 이름은 민수[예요] = My name is Minsu. ; 이에요, 있어요
f 저[는] 선생님이 아니에요 = I am not a teacher. ; 을, 에

@unit u5 | Numbers | Basics | Sino-Korean numbers, prices and rooms

t Big numbers: 십 = 10, 백 = 100, 천 = 1,000, 만 = 10,000. 삼천 원 = 3,000 won.
t 몇 asks "what number / how many": 몇 호예요? = What room number?
w 일 = one (Sino-Korean)
w 삼 = three (Sino-Korean)
w 사 = four (Sino-Korean)
w 육 = six (Sino-Korean)
w 칠 = seven (Sino-Korean)
w 팔 = eight (Sino-Korean)
w 구 = nine (Sino-Korean)
w 십 = ten (Sino-Korean)
w 백 = hundred
w 천 = thousand
w 만 = ten thousand
w 원 = won (money)
w 몇 = how many
s 이 커피는 삼천 원이에요. = This coffee is three thousand won. {nospeak}
s 이거 얼마예요? = How much is this?

s 몇 호예요? = What room number is it?

@unit u6 | Ordering food | Basics | Order in a restaurant

t 개 counts things, 잔 counts cups, 명 counts people.

@unit u7 | Everyday verbs | Everyday | The present tense
t Polite present: verb stem + 아요 (after ㅏ, ㅗ) or 어요 (after other vowels). 하다 becomes 해요.
t 을/를 marks the object: 커피를 마셔요 = I drink coffee. 를 after a vowel, 을 after a consonant.
t 에서 marks where an action happens: 도서관에서 공부해요.
w 가요 = go
w 와요 = come
w 먹어요 = eat
w 마셔요 = drink
w 봐요 = watch
w 해요 = do
w 공부해요 = study
w 일해요 = work
w 자요 = sleep
w 읽어요 = read
w 한국어 = Korean (language)
w 영화 = movie
s 저는 학교에 가요. = I go to school.
s 커피를 마셔요. = I drink coffee.
s 친구가 한국어를 공부해요. = My friend studies Korean.
s 저는 도서관에서 책을 읽어요. = I read a book at the library.
s 친구가 영화를 봐요. = My friend watches a movie.
s 지금 뭐 해요? = What are you doing now?
s 밥을 먹어요. = I eat rice.
s 형은 회사에서 일해요. = My older brother works at a company.
s 일찍 자요. = I go to bed early.
s 친구가 우리 집에 와요. = My friend comes to my house.
f 커피[를] 마셔요 = I drink coffee. ; 을, 가
f 도서관[에서] 책을 읽어요 = I read a book at the library. ; 에, 을
